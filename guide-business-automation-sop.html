Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) are the difference between a business that runs itself and a business that runs you. But most small business owners and nonprofit managers never write them — because staring at a blank page is paralyzing, and hiring a consultant costs $2,000-$10,000.

Why SOPs Matter

  • Consistency: Every task done the same way, every time, regardless of who does it
  • Onboarding: New hires or contractors productive in days, not weeks
  • Freedom: You can step away without the business falling apart
  • Valuation: Businesses with documented processes sell for 2-3x more
  • AI readiness: You can't automate what isn't documented

How to Write an SOP (Without Hating It)

  1. Record yourself doing the task. Use Loom, QuickTime, or your phone. Talk through what you're doing and why.
  2. Transcribe and edit. Turn the recording into a step-by-step document. Remove jargon.
  3. Test with someone else. Have a team member follow the SOP without your help. Where do they get stuck? Fix those steps.
  4. Set a review date. SOPs go stale. Review quarterly.
